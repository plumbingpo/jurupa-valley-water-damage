What a Professional Brings to Flood Cleanup

Getting the standing water out is only one part of cleaning up after a flood. Floodwater carries bacteria, sewage, chemicals and other contaminants that are serious health hazards. Professional flood crews are trained in containment, personal protective equipment (PPE) and sanitizing protocols that keep your family from exposure to pathogens such as E. coli, hepatitis and other waterborne diseases.

Improper cleanup also tends to miss moisture you cannot see, and within days that trapped water can cause a major mold problem. With thermal imaging, psychrometric calculations and moisture meters, trained technicians confirm that subfloors, structural framing, wall cavities and every other wet area dry to an acceptable standard. This scientific approach to drying is the only reliable way to prevent secondary damage that can cost thousands more to fix.

Flood Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Cracks in the foundation should be sealed and the basement walls given a waterproof coating
  • Being outside a designated flood zone does not rule out flooding, so think about flood coverage
  • Extend your downspouts to carry rain from the roof out past the foundation
  • Keep gutters and storm drains clear of debris so water can flow properly
  • In areas of the property that tend to flood, raise electrical systems and appliances off the floor
  • Install a battery backup sump pump so you stay protected when storms knock out power

Warning Signs for Flood Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Water coming in through foundation cracks, window wells or door thresholds
  • Soaked landscaping that leaves water pooling against your foundation
  • A layer of silt, dirt or debris on the floor after water recedes
  • Living areas flooded by contaminated water from a backed-up drain or sewer
  • Any amount of water sitting in a room, garage or basement
  • A water line on walls or baseboards showing how high it rose
  • Roof or wall openings left by a storm, with rain pouring through
